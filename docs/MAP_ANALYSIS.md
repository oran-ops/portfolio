# How an isometric city is actually built — analysis before design

Written because the last four rounds were me assembling elements on top of something basic and
then being told to improve it. That is the wrong loop. This is the analysis that should have
come first: what the reference images are actually made of, and what has to exist before any
of it can be built.

---

## 1. What the references have that my build does not

Reading images A–F, and F above all, the difference is not detail. It is **six systems** that
are all present in them and all absent from mine.

### 1.1 Buildings form BLOCKS, not objects

In every reference, buildings are joined. They share party walls and form a continuous built
edge around a block, with the open ground inside the block, not between the buildings. What
separates one building from the next is a change of façade — height, colour, window rhythm —
not a gap of grass.

My build places detached objects along a line with grass between them. That is a suburb of
identical villas, and it is why it reads as "elements on a map".

### 1.2 The pavement is a real object

Every street in the references is: carriageway → kerb → pavement → building line. The pavement
is drawn, it is continuous, and everything on the street — people, lamps, signs, trees, bins —
stands **on it**. My streets are a strip of asphalt on grass with things scattered near them.

### 1.3 Building TYPES are distinguishable at a glance

This is the note Oran made most sharply, and it is the heart of it. A city reads because a
tower cannot be mistaken for a warehouse. Each type has a **signature** made of five things:

| Type | Footprint | Height | Roof | Façade | What surrounds it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tower** | narrow, square-ish | 8–25 floors | flat, plant, mast | full-height glazing, vertical | plaza, forecourt, drop-off |
| **Mid-rise block** | wide rectangle | 4–8 floors | flat, parapet | regular window grid, **balconies** | parking court, bin store |
| **Terrace / townhouse** | narrow, deep | 2–4 floors | parapet or shallow pitch | door on the street, bay windows | no gap to neighbour |
| **Detached house** | small, square | 1–2 floors | **pitched**, chimney | few windows, front door, porch | garden, fence, driveway, car |
| **Retail podium** | wide, shallow | 1–2 floors | flat, plant | **continuous glazed shopfront + signage band** | awnings, stalls, crowd |
| **Industrial shed** | very wide, very deep | 1 tall floor | **shallow pitch, roof lights** | almost blind walls, **big roller doors** | yard, hardstanding, stacks, lorries, forklifts |

A shed is not a short block. It is wider, blinder, has a different roof and stands in a yard.
If those five things are not different, no amount of detail will separate them.

### 1.4 The life layer is systematic, not sprinkled

In the references, life is placed **by rule against the street**, not scattered:

- a car every 15–25 m in each running lane, plus parked cars along the kerb
- pedestrians on the pavement, in ones and twos, denser near shops
- a street tree every 12–18 m, in a pit in the pavement
- a lamp post every 25–30 m, alternating sides
- traffic lights and painted crossings **at every junction**
- signage: shop fascias, hanging signs, road signs, direction signs
- rooftop plant on every flat roof

The test: pick any 50 m of street. In a reference there are 8–15 objects on it. In my build
there are one or two.

### 1.5 Colour is natural, and it varies per building

Concrete grey, brick, render, sand, timber, glass — with each building slightly different in
tone, weathering and window tint. Saturated colour appears only in **small things**: cars,
awnings, signs, market stalls, doors.

I did the opposite: I applied a brand colour across the buildings. Oran's instruction is to
reverse it — **everything natural first**, and he will point at what to colour afterwards.

### 1.6 One sun, hard shadows, no atmosphere

Crisp. No fog, no aerial perspective, no soft dissolve. This part my build already does.

## 2. What has to be built, in order

Nothing below is decoration. Each item is a prerequisite for the one after it.

1. **A street section.** Carriageway, kerb, pavement, building line — as geometry, generated
   for every street. Until this exists nothing can stand in the right place.
2. **A block system.** Streets enclose blocks; each block has a perimeter and an interior.
   Buildings occupy the perimeter with party walls; the interior takes yards and parking.
3. **A typology library.** Six types, each with its own model set, footprint rule, height
   rule, roof rule and surroundings rule — from the table in §1.3.
4. **A natural colour system.** Per-type material families, per-instance variation in tone,
   weathering and window tint. No brand colour anywhere yet.
5. **A street-furniture system.** The rules in §1.4, applied to every street automatically, so
   density is a property of the system and not of my patience.
6. **Junctions.** Painted crossings, signals, give-way lines wherever two streets meet.
7. **Signage.** Fascias on every shopfront, road signs on every approach.

## 3. What this means for the four places

Each place is then a different **mix of types and densities**, which is what will actually make
them feel different — not a different colour:

| | dominant types | density | signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **XTIX** | towers + mid-rise, 30 % of plots as sites | very high | cranes, skeletons, hoardings among finished towers |
| **OASIS** | detached houses + terraces + one retail podium | medium | gardens, fences, cars on drives, children, playground |
| **EVENTER** | mid-rise + retail, wrapped around the interchange | high | ramps, queued traffic, signals, signage |
| **MEDCOIN** | towers in the centre, industrial sheds at the edge | high centre, low edge | suits vs hi-vis; glass vs corrugated; plaza vs yard |

---

## 4. The honest position

My builds so far have step 5 (some scattered furniture) without steps 1–4. That is why more
detail has not helped: there is no street section for it to stand on, no block for it to
belong to, and no typology for it to differentiate.

The work is to build 1–4 first. That is a real piece of engineering, not a tweak, and it is
what the references are made of.
