# MEDCOIN — the build plan

Written 2026-08-25, before any geometry. Everything here is derived from what OASIS *actually
became* — measured, not remembered — plus the vision brief. Nothing is guessed.

**Rule carried over and binding: approve the plan, then build. No designing ahead of agreement,
and every stage is measured and shown before the next one starts.**

---

## 1. What MEDCOIN is

From `MAP_VISION.md` §5, in Oran's words:

> Like the Ramat Gan exchange, like the Twin Towers district. Work, industry, companies —
> little residential. Office buildings, real skyscrapers if they serve.
> **At the edges:** low industry — factories, tractors, forklifts, material yards, smoke,
> concrete, coal, stone, marble, timber, metal, **people working with their hands**.
> **At the centre:** skyscrapers, offices, **people in suits** — law firms, finance — and the
> restaurants that go with that world.

The organising idea: **a hard gradient from hands to suits.** The edge is low, loud, dusty and
busy; the centre is tall, glazed and expensive. A reader should be able to point anywhere and
say which world it belongs to. That contrast IS the content — the way "finished and lived-in"
was the content of OASIS.

## 2. Frame and proportions — inherited from OASIS, measured

OASIS as built, measured 2026-08-25:

| | OASIS as built |
|---|---|
| city square | 372 x 372 m = 138,384 m2 |
| buildings | **151** |
| secondary street length | **4,577 m** = 330.7 m per hectare |
| main road through it | 922 m |
| streets | 9 horizontal + 15 vertical = 24, **66 junctions** |
| traffic | **190** vehicles |
| people | **298** |
| vegetation | 6,426 trees, 3,650 bushes, 713 flowers, 655 grass |
| street furniture | 93 lamps, 8 signals, 11 benches, 256 fence panels, 39 parked cars |

**MEDCOIN uses the same 372 x 372 m frame and the same 8 m grid unit** — same share of the map
(15 %), same road widths (main 26.2 m dual, secondary 10 m). That keeps the four cities reading
as one family and lets every constant and tool carry over unchanged.

### Density targets, derived from OASIS's own measured districts

OASIS came out as:

| district | coverage | buildings/ha | median height |
|---|---|---|---|
| HOUSES | 19.8 % | 17.2 | 6.4 m |
| CENTRE | 20.3 % | 18.1 | 9.3 m |
| TOWERS | 28.5 % | 23.2 | 29.4 m |
| BLOCKS | 9.7 % | 6.8 | 9.8 m |

MEDCOIN is **far** denser and taller — a business district, not a suburb. Oran's direction,
2026-08-25: *the two inner zones very dense, the two outer zones less dense but with no dead
space at all — no forest and no empty green inside the city. Coverage around 60–70 % of the
built area. 151 buildings is nowhere near enough.*

### The arithmetic — revised 2026-08-25 after Oran's density review

Oran's challenge was correct and worth recording: **OASIS's built-to-green ratio is nothing like
what MEDCOIN needs.** Measured, OASIS is:

| OASIS, measured | share of the 372 m square |
|---|---|
| building footprint (17,049 m2) | **12.3 %** |
| streets, net | ~27 % |
| **green — gardens, park, verges** | **~60 %** |

Multiplying a 12.3 % number by 3.7 still leaves a loose city. MEDCOIN inverts the ratio
entirely: **zero green inside the square.**

Two further faults were found while checking, both real:

1. **The zone geometry did not match the stated shares.** The first concentric layout gave the
   CORE 9.7 % and the INDUSTRY BELT 62.8 %, against a plan that claimed 26 % and 34 %. In a
   concentric layout the outer ring always dominates — the maths has to be done, not assumed.
2. **The street grid was too dense to build on.** 7,818 m of street in a 372 m square is a 35 m
   average spacing; minus a 10 m carriageway that leaves **25 m of block depth**, which cannot
   take buildings on both sides. The grid is thinned to ~48–55 m spacing.

### Final geometry — belt 52 m, approved

The belt width was tested against what actually has to fit inside it:

| belt width | belt share | strip each side of the service road | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35 m | 34.1 % | 12.5 m | too tight for anything |
| 45 m | 42.5 % | 17.5 m | yards only, no sheds |
| **52 m** | **48.1 %** | **21 m** | **shed 20 m deep + yard — chosen** |
| 68 m | 59.8 % | 29 m | belt swallows the city |

**52 m**: a 10 m service road down the middle, a 21 m strip outside it and a 21 m strip inside.
A 20 x 35 m shed sits in either strip with its long axis along the belt; the 31.7 m gantry crane
fits the same way. That is the room Oran asked to keep for the industrial elements.

| **INDUSTRY BELT** | 52 m ring | 66,560 | 48.1 % |

| zone | block coverage | avg footprint | **buildings** |

| CORE | 70 % | 70 m2 (narrow towers, party walls) | **180** |
| OFFICE RING | 65 % | 90 m2 | **175** |

| | OASIS | **MEDCOIN** |
|---|---|---|
| buildings | 151 | **550** — 3.6x |
| building footprint | 12.3 % of city | **~37 %** |
| streets | 4,577 m | ~5,600 m |

**How the square adds to 100 % with no dead space:** buildings 37 % + streets 27 % + yards,

**Why 550 and not 800:** at 800 the average plot drops below 12 m of frontage and the district
stops reading as a city — it becomes one continuous wall. 550 narrow buildings with party walls
is what a real financial district looks like.

**People sanity check:** 5,600 m of street gives 11,200 m of pavement. 1,200 people is one every
9.3 m — a busy business district at lunchtime. Real, not absurd.

**Technical budget:** OASIS carries 12,631 objects, of which **11,492 are vegetation**. MEDCOIN
has almost none, so that budget goes to buildings, people, vehicles and industrial elements.
Estimate ~5,300 objects — **lighter than OASIS**.

## 2b. Variety and loading — Oran's three requirements, 2026-08-25

These are not decoration. Each is a build rule with a numeric test.

### A. The CORE must be varied in everything

Oran: varied in building type, in heights, in design, in colours, in windows, in roofs — in
everything.

| axis | how |
|---|---|
| **model** | every tower model in the library — 5 `building-skyscraper-a…e`, `building-l`, `building-m`, `building-148`, 4 `building-sample-tower-a…d`, plus the donor city's 4 skyscrapers = **16 distinct models** |
| **height** | per-instance vertical scale **0.7x – 1.9x**, giving an apparent range of **22 m to 78 m** from those 16 models |
| **colour** | per-instance hue and value shift from a position hash, inside the family — the same machinery that graded the vegetation into three bands and recoloured the donor houses |
| **windows** | model choice already varies the glazing rhythm; glazed and solid families are deliberately mixed along each street rather than clustered |
| **roofs** | flat roofs get **rooftop plant** — tanks, aerials, AC units, `detail-tank`, `machine_barrel`, `mast`, `radio` — so no two roofs read the same from above |
| **plot** | narrow plots with party walls, widths staggered 8–18 m, so the skyline steps rather than marches |

**Test:** no two adjacent buildings share a model; height standard deviation across the CORE
above 12 m; at least 60 % of flat roofs carry plant.

### B. The CIVIC / DINING strip must be dense and dynamic

Oran: very dense and very dynamic in its businesses — no fixedness of size, of design, or of
business type.

The donor city carries **71 unique shop models** — cafés, ice-cream, fruit, vegetables, bakery,
sport, plants, gaming, cheese. Every one gets used.

| axis | how |
|---|---|
| **business type** | all 71 donor shops plus `food-kit` and `retro-urban-kit` frontages |
| **size** | unit widths deliberately staggered 4–14 m; no two neighbours the same width |
| **height** | 1 to 3 storeys mixed along the same parade, not sorted |
| **design** | shopfronts, awnings, signage bands and planters vary per unit |
| **rhythm** | continuous frontage with party walls, broken by occasional 6 m gaps for alleys and outdoor seating |

**Test:** at least 30 distinct shop models used; no model repeated within 4 units; unit-width
standard deviation above 2.5 m.

### C. The INDUSTRY BELT must be loaded with elements, not buildings

Oran: loaded, but not necessarily with buildings like the centre — more with elements such as
lorries, rocks, tractors, yards and cranes.

The belt carries only 85 buildings but **~900 industrial elements**:

| forklifts | industry pack | 20 |
| containers, crates | 28 models | 140 |

**Cars stay dense in the belt** — Oran was explicit about this. Only the **people** drop, to

**Test:** belt element count above 850; zero patches of bare grass; every yard has ground
treatment (hardstanding, hazard paint or bare earth) and at least three element types.

### Where MEDCOIN is built

OASIS now occupies 372 x 372 m of the 700 x 650 m world, which is 76 % of the map — there is no
room for a second city beside it. **MEDCOIN is therefore built in its own file,
`world-build/medcoin.blend`**, on an identical 372 x 372 frame with identical conventions
(8 m unit, same road widths, same sun rig, same palette). Composing the four cities onto the
final master map is a later stage, exactly as `MAP_VISION.md` section 7 anticipates. This also
keeps each file light enough to work in — world_map.blend already carries 12,631 objects.

## 2c. Plan audit — self-check run 2026-08-25, before any geometry

Oran asked for a hard re-check of every ratio and of whether anything was forgotten. The
arithmetic was recomputed rather than re-asserted, and the plan was cross-read against
`MAP_ANALYSIS.md`, `BUGLOG.md`, `WORLD_STATE.md`, `NIGHT_BUILD_PLAN.md` and `PLANTING_PLAN.md`.

### What passed

Zone areas sum to exactly 138,384 m2 (100.0 %). Every zone's building count multiplied by its
average footprint agrees with its stated coverage to within **3 %**. Vehicle density comes to one
per 24.5 m per lane — busy but not a jam. Grid spacing at 5,600 m of street gives 39 m of block
depth, enough for two rows of buildings plus a service gap.

### Fault 1 — THERE IS NO PAVEMENT. The biggest finding.

`MAP_ANALYSIS.md` section 1.2 named this as one of six systems the reference images have and our
build lacks: *"Every street in the references is carriageway, kerb, pavement, building line. The
pavement is drawn, it is continuous, and everything on the street stands on it."*

**It was never built.** The OASIS street as constructed is:

```
   carriageway 7.4 m  |  kerb 1.3 m  |  ...nothing
```

In OASIS the grass absorbed it — people stood at 6.3 m from the centreline, which is on grass,
not on a pavement. **In MEDCOIN, where the rule is zero green, buildings would sit on bare ground
with a 1.3 m kerb between them and the traffic.** It would be glaring.

**Fix — a real street section, generated for every street:**

| element | width |
|---|---|
| carriageway | 6.4 m (two 3.2 m lanes) |
| kerb | 0.5 m each side |
| **pavement** | **2.3 m each side, its own surface at z = 0.19** |
| **corridor** | **12.0 m** (was 10.0) |

Main road: 23.6 m carriageway + 0.5 m kerb + 3.0 m pavement each side = 30.6 m.

Everything on the street now stands **on the pavement**: people, lamps, signals, trees in pits,
bus shelters, signage. That is section 1.2 finally satisfied.

### Fault 2 — the belt would have looked sparse, not loaded

900 elements over 30,900 m2 of belt yard is one element per 34 m2 — a **17 % visual fill**. That
reads as empty hardstanding, the opposite of what Oran asked for.

**Fix: 1,400 elements**, weighted to chunky items — container stacks, material heaps, timber
stacks, pallet blocks — averaging ~10 m2 instead of ~6. That gives **42 % yard fill: loaded.**

### Fault 3 — the belt's people were cut too hard

Oran said reduce the volume of people in the belt *a little*. The plan had cut it to a third,
giving one person per 32.7 m — a dead street. **Fix: the belt takes 20 % of the 1,200 people, one
per 19.6 m.** The core stays at one per 5.1 m.

### Fault 4 — four sub-systems were missing from the plan entirely

| missing | added |
|---|---|
| **painted crossings** at junctions | zebra bars on every arm of every major junction, white, z = 0.152 |
| **traffic signals** named explicitly | signals on all four corners of the 8 busiest junctions |
| **bus stops and shelters** | `bus-stop-1` on the ring roads, 6 stops with waiting figures |
| **signage on offices, not just shops** | banner and sign families on the office and tower frontages, so the district reads as commercial |

### Fault 5 — two import lessons had not been carried into the plan

| lesson | why it matters here |
|---|---|
| **OBJ import puts the +90 deg X rotation on the OBJECT, not the mesh.** Every mesh-space measurement — asphalt height, kerb side, which face is the front — must go through `matrix_world`. | The whole industry pack and every kit model arrives this way |
| **`colormap` materials carry `Kd 1 1 1`** and hold the real colour in the texture. Sorting materials by `diffuse_color` luminance picks garbage; sample the palette texture at a real face UV instead. | This is how the road asphalt colour was found in OASIS; the same is needed for the industry pack |

### Corrected budget — the city square, 100 % accounted for

The main road runs **outside** the 372 m square, along its west and north edges, so its corridor
does not consume city land.

| | m2 | share |
|---|---|---|
| buildings | 50,300 | **36.3 %** |
| streets + pavements | 45,600 | **33.0 %** |
| yards, plazas, car parks, loading bays | 42,484 | **30.7 %** |
| **grass** | **0** | **0.0 %** |
| sum | 138,384 | 100.0 % |

**Belt:** 66,560 m2 = 15,960 streets + 17,000 buildings + **33,600 m2 of yard**, of which 1,400
elements fill 42 %.

**Pavement:** 25,760 m2 across 11,200 m of edge. 1,200 people is one per 9.3 m of pavement —
core one per 5.1 m, office one per 9 m, belt one per 19.6 m.

| my test said | his eye said |

Every one of these techniques already exists — the black-streak hunt, the grass calibration and
the floating audit all used exactly this. **The change is to run them before showing Oran, not

### PART 2 — The shadow, analysed properly against buildings

#### A mistake I made, found by re-reading the record

1. The sun sat at 30 degrees. Shadows were 1.73x object height. Oran: things look like they float.
2. I raised it to 68 degrees. Oran looked again: still floating.
3. I then found the real cause — **his viewport had `use_scene_lights = False` and was lighting
   the map with `forest.exr`. He had never actually seen the 68-degree result.**
4. But by then I had already pushed the sun to 82 degrees.

**82 degrees was chosen to fix a symptom whose real cause turned out to be the viewport. Two
variables were changed at once.** That is worth stating plainly, because it cost something.

#### The detachment rule, derived rather than guessed

A shadow reads as *detached* only when the shadow of an object's widest part lands outside its own
footprint. That can only happen to objects with a **narrow base and a wide top**.

- **tree**: canopy centre ~5 m up, canopy radius ~2 m → attached while `5·cot(elev) < 2` → **elev > 68.2°**
- **buildings are prisms.** Their shadow always starts at their own base. **A building can never
  look detached, at any sun angle.**

**The floating problem was only ever about trees and small props.** MEDCOIN is 550 buildings and
about 180 trees — almost entirely prisms.

#### What each elevation costs and buys

| elev | tree offset | verdict | 38 m tower shadow | 78 m tower shadow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 68° | 2.02 m | **tree detaches** | 15.4 m | 31.5 m |
| 72° | 1.62 m | attached | 12.3 m | 25.3 m |
| **75°** | **1.34 m** | **attached** | **10.2 m** | **20.9 m** |
| 78° | 1.06 m | attached | 8.1 m | 16.6 m |
| 82° (current) | 0.70 m | attached | 5.3 m | 11.0 m |

#### Recommendation: **75 degrees**

- Tree offset 1.34 m, comfortably inside the 2 m canopy — **no floating, the fix holds.**
- A 38 m tower throws a **10.2 m** shadow: it crosses most of a 12 m street and climbs the
  opposite facade. That is the CBD canyon look — and it is **how a viewer tells a 30 m tower from
  a 50 m one when looking down from an isometric camera.**
- At 82° that same tower throws only 5.3 m, and two towers of different heights look nearly
  identical from above.

**Why this matters more for MEDCOIN than it did for OASIS:** OASIS's content was low and its
height range was 4.8–11 m, so shadow carried almost no information. MEDCOIN's whole subject is
**height** — a range of 22 to 78 m. Throwing away the shadow throws away the height cue.

75° buys the height-reading back at zero floating risk. **Sun energy is rescaled by
cos(8°)/cos(15°) = 0.955 to keep the measured ground values unchanged.**

#### Sun azimuth versus camera — checked, not assumed

The camera sits at offset (+520, −520, +520): to the south-east, looking north-west. It therefore
sees the **south and east faces** of every building. With azimuth 135° the light comes from the
north-east, so **the east face is lit and the south face is shaded** — every building shows one
lit and one shaded face. That is correct modelling. **Keep azimuth 135°.**

### PART 3 — Micro-resolution: the z-stack, to the millimetre

OASIS lost time twice to coplanar surfaces rendering black, and both cures were ad hoc. For
MEDCOIN the stack is **fixed in advance** and every surface has its own height.

| surface | z (m) | note |
|---|---|---|
| terrain / green country | 0.000 | outside the square only |
| bare earth, material yards | 0.030 | belt yards |
| ground plates — car park, plaza | 0.050 | as OASIS |
| hazard floor paint | 0.062 | sits on the plate, +12 mm |
| cul-de-sac disc | 0.132 | **8 mm below** asphalt so the street wins |
| carriageway asphalt | 0.140 | as OASIS |
| road markings, dashes, zebra | 0.152 | +12 mm, proven clear |
| **pavement surface** | **0.190** | **new** — 10 mm below the kerb top |
| kerb top | 0.200 | the 10 mm lip is what reads as a kerb |
| **building base** | **0.190** | **buildings sit ON the pavement, not on the ground** |
| people and props on pavement | 0.210 | pavement + 20 mm |
| cars on carriageway | 0.285 | the proven OASIS value |
| cars parked on a plate | 0.110 | plate + 60 mm |

**Rule: no two surfaces that can overlap in plan may share a z. Minimum separation 8 mm.**

**The building-base line was never specified anywhere.** Left at 0, the new pavement would have
driven straight through the base of all 550 buildings.

#### Party walls — a z-fight nobody has hit yet

The plan calls for party walls. Two buildings whose side walls are exactly coplanar **will z-fight
along the entire shared wall** — the same failure as the black streak, but vertical and 38 m tall.

**Fix: a 0.06 m gap between adjacent buildings.** At 4.03 px per metre that is **0.24 px —
genuinely invisible** — and it is 1/200th of a 12 m plot, so the parade still reads as continuous.

At 1500 px over 372 m the render gives **4.03 px per metre**:

|---|---|---|

| kerb height 0.20 m | 0.81 | a line — exactly its job |
| road dash 0.36 m | 1.45 | a line |
| person 0.6 m wide | 2.42 | reads clearly |

## 6. Every lesson from OASIS, applied here in advance

These are mistakes already paid for. Each line is a rule for MEDCOIN, not a note.

**Placement**

1. A street corridor is a **segment**, not an infinite line. Clearance tests must use the
   street's real extent or valid parcels get killed.
2. The main road is **not vertical at every y**. Compute its x at each street's own y before
   trimming, or streets cross it with no junction — the bB bug, 44 m of road.
3. Align every frontage row to **one building line**, plus a second line 12 m behind for the
   second rank. Ragged setbacks read as "diagonal" even when rotation is exactly 0.
4. Claim rectangles come from the model's **real footprint**, and every placement is re-checked
   after a move.
5. **Never place a small object on a plate without checking the plate** — five shops ended up
   standing on a car park.
6. Kit names lie: `building-30/q/s` are open canopy frames, not shops. Judge by the render
   sheet, always.

**Geometry**

7. Coplanar faces at the same z **z-fight and render black**. Fixed heights: terrain 0,
   plates 0.05, asphalt 0.14, kerb 0.20, road markings 0.152.
8. Do not build a patch where two surfaces already overlap — it z-fights with itself.
9. Author flat plates with **explicit winding** (cross.z > 0). `recalc_face_normals` does not
   orient open plates.
10. One object's bbox can **lie about its coverage** — the 3-strip plaza plate had a 144 m bbox
    and silently blocked every placement in the band. Split multi-part plates into separate
    objects.

**Vehicles**

11. Each car model has its own **base rotation** — some are modelled along X, some along Y.
    Calibrate `pre` per mesh once, then always set `rotation = heading + pre`. Writing the yaw
    directly destroys it; that is how 77 of 190 cars ended up broadside across the road.

**Light and colour**

12. **Sun elevation drives the whole map's credibility.** At 30 degrees every shadow is 1.73x
    the object's height and everything reads as floating. MEDCOIN keeps the corrected rig:
    **elevation 75 degrees (shadow 0.268x height), sun disc 3.4 degrees, energy 0.820** — see section 2e part 2 for why 82 was an over-correction.
13. Measure the render against the target; do not eyeball it. The grass was 34 % too dark for
    weeks and nobody could see it until it was measured.
14. **Check the viewport settings before believing a fault.** The floating shadows that survived
    the sun fix were the viewport lighting the scene with `forest.exr` and
    `use_scene_lights = False`. The render had been correct the whole time.

**Process**

15. `view_layer.update()` per placement on a large scene costs 1–2 s. Precompute each
    prototype's bbox, centre offset and ground drop once, then place with pure arithmetic.
16. The MCP socket times out at 300 s but the work completes. Poll the output files; never
    resend the same call.
17. Objects removed from `bpy.data` must never be touched again. Track by name and re-get.
18. Save after every stage. Blender crashed twice; both times the file was intact.
