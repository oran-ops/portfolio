The seven, on both grounds

Every icon twice: on the white of an open window, and on the 50 per cent checkerboard of the desktop. The second row is the test that matters — it is where a paper-white icon goes missing.

32 × 32, shown at ×2

Why each one looks the way it does

  • Row breakThe first row is the four companies, in the order they happened. The second is everything that is not a company — and READ ME sits at the end of it, where a reader finishes rather than starts. On a phone the same break lands as four rows of two, with READ ME alone at the bottom.
  • Four case filesThey share one silhouette on purpose: they are the same kind of thing, and colour is what separates them. The three that are not companies differ in outline alone — which is how a desktop said “different kind of thing” without a word of explanation.
  • MEDCOINPaper white, which would vanish on a white window — so its keyline is drawn twice. That is the period answer to a light icon. The fix is in the idiom, not in the palette; the colour is untouched.
  • SelectionA 1-bit Finder selected by inverting the bitmap. On a colour icon that turns emerald into magenta, so this dims inside the mask and inverts only the label — which is what System 7 did once icons had colour.
  • The chromeThe title bar is built to the measured System 1 figures: 1 black, 3 white, 11 striped, 3 white, 1 black, with the title punched through the stripes with 6 px of white either side. Scroll bars are present and empty — in 1-bit there is no greying, and that absence is the inactive state.
  • Nothing borrowedChicago, Geneva and the System 6 icons are Apple's, and docs/KIT_OPTIONS.md rules them out. The face here is drawn from scratch on a 5 × 7 cell and the icons on a 32 × 32 grid, so the whole kit is ours — menu mark included.

Two places the machine lost the argument

Both of these were open questions. Both are now settled, and in both the reader won over the period.

  • The countIt reads 7 files · 0 of 7 opened · 2018–2026. The earlier figure of six treated READ ME as something other than a file; it is a file, a reader opens it like the rest, and a Finder counted what the window held. A wrong count is the one thing a Mac user spots instantly. Both numbers are now read off the icon list itself, so they cannot drift from what is on the screen.
  • The close boxSystem 1 drew this square empty and filled it on press. It carries an X instead, deliberately: an empty square does not read as an exit to anyone who never used the machine. Clarity beat authenticity, which is the right way round — the shell exists to be understood, not to pass an inspection.
